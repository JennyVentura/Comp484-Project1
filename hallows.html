Although discussed earlier, I'd like to dive a bit deeper into All Hallows' Eve as it also has a tradition that carried over to modern Halloween.

Trick-Or-Treat?

Earlier, though we spoke about All Saints Day and All Hallows' Eve, there is also something called All Souls Day. All Souls day comes after All Saints Day and is the day that we honor the souls of people who went to heaven before us. During this day. in the past, the poor would go door to door asking for a treat called a souls cake in exchange for a prayer for the dead. This was it, this was essentially the beginning of trick-or-treat!

I feel it's important to note that while All Hallows' Eve is no longer observed by the Catholic church, it is still used as a preparation night for All Saints day.

How Did It Get Here?

Okay, so now we know how Halloween started and why we do some of the things we do, but how did it get to the U.S.? Halloween was introduced to the United States when the British and Irish first came here, but it wasn't widely celebrated until the 1840s during the Great Potato Famine, which brought an influx of people celebrating the tradtion. In the U.S., Halloween instead became a time for parties and pranking. And in the 1930s, trick-or-treat was introduced as an alternative to children's pranking activity. And that's how it all came to be.
