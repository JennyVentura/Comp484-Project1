Early History

Originally, Halloween was not Halloween. A long time ago, it used to be a Celtic celebration called Samhain (pronounced Saa-wn or sow-in). This celebration began at the beginning of winter and marked the first day of the New Year for the Celts. It was a time that was believed to be when the barrier between our world and the next was incredibly thin, and as such, passage between the two was seen as possible. They believed that non-human entities and spirits were able to more easily cross over and would be around to visit. Strange creatures, some believed to lure in and harm people, and the ghosts of the dead were dangerous and not something they wanted to be percieved by. In order to avoid being attacked and/or killed by these entities, the Celts would dress up to hide amongst them, making it more difficult for the spirits to spot them. This is thankfully one of the traditions that has made it all the way to modern times.

What Changed?

Although Samhain was very old tradition going back many centuries, it was sadly attacked for its pegan roots and practices when the Catholic church began Christianization efforts in Ireland. Though the Catholic church disliked the practice of pagnism and pushed for its end along with the conversion of people to Christianity, it had no probloem absorbing the holiday and changing it to suit its needs instead. Thus, Samhain was changed into Halloween, an occasion for church feasts and holy days. The first day of November being the Feast of All Saints, or All Hallows, and the day before, October 31st, becoming the Eve of All Hallows, Hallow Evening or All Hallows' Eve, which later became known as Hallowe'en (shortened for our modern convenience).

Old Traditions Still Seen Today

To list a few traditions still seen today:

  • Dressing up in costumes (as discussed earlier)
  • Leaving treats out for spirits (although today this is done for trick-or-treaters)
  • Bobbing for apples (a tradition that showed up when the Romans did)
  • Carving Jack-O-Lanterns

On a side note, originally Jack-O-Lanterns were actually carved turnips. They were believed to ward off evil from the home. The only reason Americans carve pumpkins instead is because when Halloween made its way to the New World, squash were abundant.
